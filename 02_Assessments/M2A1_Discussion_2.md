# Discussion 2
Slate Order or Frame Order

M2A1  |  Individual  |  10 points  |  End of Week 2

Use a real decision setting to explain how reversing an order could change the answer. Design a comparison that preserves the full answer pattern and gives a competing explanation a fair test.

## Prepare Your Post

Slate order concerns the sequence of alternatives or items under consideration. Frame order concerns the sequence of ways of evaluating the same decision, such as convenience and privacy. A rival explanation is another process that could produce the observed difference, such as memory, learning, or carryover from an earlier answer.

1. Identify a company decision and link a public source for the setting. State whether your proposed comparison concerns slate order, frame order, or both. Explain the classification using the actual items.

2. Write two brief framing questions, A and B, and one final yes-or-no decision question, F. Show the two sequences: A then B then F, and B then A then F. Explain why the two frames could compete.

3. Explain how you would make the groups comparable and keep the wording and background information the same. State the order effect you would calculate from F and the paired A and B answers you would retain for the QQ calculation.

4. Name the strongest rival and one additional observation it predicts. State why an interval for q that contains zero can still leave the QQ check unresolved. Describe a result that would change your initial interpretation.

## Submission

Post 300–400 words in the Canvas discussion and write one substantive peer response of about 100–150 words. Sources and a brief AI disclosure may sit outside the word count. Use the exact dates shown in Canvas; allow time to respond to a classmate.

## Peer Response and Assessment

Read a peer’s two sequences. Identify one change besides order that could enter their comparison. Suggest a control or a more balanced wording, and explain how it would improve the interpretation.

## Use of AI

AI Assessment Scale level 2. AI may help draft the post, but you must check the claims, revise the reasoning in your own words, and take responsibility for the final text. Add a brief note identifying the tool and what you changed, or state that you did not use AI. Apply the same rule to the peer response.
