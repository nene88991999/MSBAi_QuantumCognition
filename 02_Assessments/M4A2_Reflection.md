# Reflection on Analytical Judgment

M4A2  |  Individual  |  10 points  |  10% of Part 1  |  5% of course  |  Week 4

Explain one consequential change in your judgment during the project and the evidence that prompted it. Use a specific before-and-after example to show how your own reasoning developed within the team project. Identify your contribution and distinguish your judgment from the team’s shared conclusion.

## Write an 800 Word Memo

1. State your initial judgment. Identify the company decision, your prediction, and the assumption that mattered most. Point to the dated Part 1 passage or design choice that records it.

2. Describe what challenged or refined that judgment. Use a result, design problem, rival explanation, or peer critique. Explain why it mattered, with one or two specific pieces of evidence.

3. State your revised judgment and its consequence. The change may concern the predicted size, the mechanism, the evidence tier, the design, or the recommended action. If you retained the original conclusion, explain what you tested and why the evidence justified retaining it.

4. Explain one reasoning habit you would use in a future decision. Describe a concrete situation, the step you would take, and the observation that would cause you to reconsider. Relate the habit to your handling of AI output and your own responsibility for the conclusion.

## Submission

Enter the memo in Canvas. Aim for 800 words; 720–880 words is a reasonable working range. Cite the relevant part of the team project and any external source you use. Each student submits an independently written memo, including when team members discuss the same finding. References and a brief AI disclosure may sit outside the word count. Write a connected argument rather than a diary of tasks completed.

## Reflection Rubric

| Criterion | Points | Evidence for Full Credit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial Judgment | 2 | Identifies a specific prior claim and its assumption, with a traceable project reference. |
| Evidence and Revision | 4 | Explains how concrete evidence changed or justified retaining the judgment, and states the consequence for the decision. |
| Transfer to Future Work | 3 | Develops one usable reasoning habit through a concrete future situation, action, and review condition; explains the student’s responsibility for AI-supported work. |
| Clarity and Attribution | 1 | Provides a coherent memo near the word guide, accurate references, and the required AI disclosure. |

Total: 10 points. Score each criterion using the anchors below; half points may be used. The direction of your finding does not affect the score.

Full credit: accurate, complete, and supported. About three quarters: sound reasoning with a minor omission. About half: a substantial omission or error weakens the argument. About one quarter: a relevant attempt with little support. Zero: absent or unrelated. Apply these anchors within each criterion, rather than counting words mechanically.

## Use of AI

AI Assessment Scale level 1. AI may help brainstorm possible topics before you write. Write and revise the submitted prose yourself. Briefly identify any brainstorming tool and how you used it, or state that you did not use AI.

Learning objectives: M4LO3 and M4LO5. There is no required direction of change and no requirement to claim that the original prediction was wrong.
