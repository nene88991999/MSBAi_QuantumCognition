# Project Part 1
Research Proposal and Audit Design

M2A2  |  Individual  |  25 points  |  End of Week 2

Choose a real company decision and design an audit of AI-generated respondents before collecting results. Your proposal should explain why the questions could affect answers, what the competing explanations predict, and how the result would inform the company’s next step.

## The Decision and the Questions

Use public evidence about a real company, including your employer if appropriate. Frame the question yourself before consulting AI. A synthetic panel is a collection of responses generated by a model under fictional respondent descriptions. It can help test a survey process; evidence about real customers requires a separate human study.

1. Describe the management decision and the outcome the company cares about. Use at least two public sources, preferably including a company or institutional source. Identify what those sources establish and what remains your hypothesis.

2. Explain why a synthetic panel could be useful for this stage, such as checking question wording before a human study. Identify the population a later human study would need to reach.

3. Choose two frames, meaning two ways of evaluating the same decision. Ground each in the company setting. Write a yes-or-no question for frame A, a yes-or-no question for frame B, and a final yes-or-no outcome question F. Define which answer is coded 1.

4. Predict how reversing A and B could change F. Name the strongest rival explanation and a further observation that would help test it. Explain why the rival is plausible; a proposal does not need to dismiss it in advance.

## Submission

Submit a proposal of about 1,000–1,300 words, plus the question battery, design table, public sources, and AI Attribution Log. Use the Project Templates and Collection Guide to prepare the attachments. Upload Word or PDF in Canvas and include the repository link you will use for the notebook and supporting files. ## Plan the Comparisons Before Collection

Use the same fictional persona grid in both required panels. The isolated-item baseline asks each question in a fresh conversation. The constructed panel asks one question at a time and retains earlier questions and answers. The ordinary all-at-once panel shown in the lectures is optional.

1. Specify A then B then F and B then A then F for the constructed panel. Give the baseline the same two order labels while keeping every question isolated. Retain both framing answers and the outcome from each complete interview.

2. Add a direct condition that asks F alone and an assessed condition that asks A then F with the A answer retained. Give both the same background facts. These extra conditions supply the interference and bracket comparisons; reversing A and B alone does not.

3. Describe the persona grid, number of interviews, model and settings, randomized run schedule, coding rule, retry limit, and treatment of missing responses. Explain your sample and cost plan. A small pilot can reveal problems, but uncertainty may require an inconclusive verdict.

4. State a meaningful threshold for the F order effect and a separate tolerance around zero for q. Give both in percentage points. Explain the managerial basis for the first and the precision standard for the second. Use the practice notebook to explore whether the proposed sample can support the intended comparison.

5. Write decision rules for a small precise effect, a meaningful effect with q within tolerance, q clearly outside tolerance, and wide intervals. Add the direct-versus-assessed comparison and the strongest rival to each relevant branch. State an action for a flat or unresolved result.

6. Freeze a dated version of the proposal and questions before the main run. Record later changes, their reasons, and whether they occurred before or after you saw the relevant results.

The Collection Guide supplies a six-condition design and a matching notebook. You may propose another defensible design, but agree on the analysis and resampling unit with the instructor before collection. Keep the task within the approved model access and spending limit.

Full credit: accurate, complete, and supported. About three quarters: sound reasoning with a minor omission. About half: a substantial omission or error weakens the argument. About one quarter: a relevant attempt with little support. Zero: absent or unrelated. Apply these anchors within each criterion, rather than counting words mechanically.
