# Discussion 3 Cognitive Asymmetry in a Human AI Process

M3A1  |  Individual  |  10 points  |  10% of Part 1  |  5% of course  |  Week 3

Examine a workflow in which an AI system supplies information or asks for a judgment before a person acts. Explain how that step could change the decision and propose a test that supports a practical improvement.

## Prepare Your Post

Cognitive asymmetry means that people and AI systems may produce judgments through different processes. Similar answers do not establish a shared process. An intermediate judgment is an assessment made before the final action. Frame order is the sequence of evaluation questions. Orientation means the goal or evaluative perspective that the interface makes salient.

1. Describe the company, the user, and the final action. Link a public source for the workflow. Walk through the steps so a reader can see where the AI enters and where the person makes a judgment.

2. Choose one design variable: an intermediate judgment, frame order, or orientation. Explain what changes and why it could affect the final action. Keep claims about an actual product separate from your proposed mechanism.

3. Propose one controlled comparison and identify the outcome, the records needed, and the strongest competing explanation. If you propose interference, include both a direct-action condition and an assessment-then-action condition.

4. State the evidence tier. A simulation checks the calculation; model-generated responses describe the named model setup; a human study describes the sampled people under the tested conditions. Recommend one action or further test with an owner and a condition for reconsidering it.
