# Quantum Approaches for Decision Making: Course Outline

Nathan Yang · BADM 590 · Spring 2027 · 2 credits · Eight modules

The course begins with a managerial decision: how should an organization respond when the way it asks a question changes the answer? We compare models of judgment, audit an AI-supported process, and examine what choices reveal about reasons and control. No physics or philosophy background is assumed.

## Module 1: Why Classical Models Break Down

How questions reveal or construct judgments; a plain-language geometric-model bridge; three patterns and fair model comparison.

Explain a classical probability model and the fixed-response assumption. Distinguish revealing a prior judgment from constructing one during questioning. Compare order effects, interference, and failures of a common joint description. Explain the geometric model without a physics interpretation. Specify when additional predictive restrictions justify a more complex model.

Discussion 1; select and document a company decision.

## Module 2: Contextuality and Order Effects

Question order, sequential judgments, the QQ restriction, uncertainty, tolerances, and classical rivals.

Distinguish question order from frame order. Explain why reversing judgments can change a response. Interpret the QQ restriction and a plausible rival. Use intervals and practical tolerances to distinguish a small effect from an unresolved result. Design comparisons that hold relevant conditions fixed.

Discussion 2; develop the proposal and question battery.

## Module 3: Human-AI Decision Teaming

Intermediate judgments, recommender goals, synthetic-panel designs, and the scope of model-generated evidence.

Identify the intermediate judgment requested by an AI process. Explain how a recommender goal selects a version of the user’s preferences. Distinguish isolated, constructed, and all-at-once synthetic panels. Execute and interpret a practice audit with appropriate uncertainty. State the human evidence needed before relying on a synthetic panel.

Discussion 3; individual practice; team proposal due at the end of Week 3.

## Module 4: Audit Verdicts and Managerial Action

Combine the audit statistics, assess rivals, document revision, and prepare an evidence-based recommendation.

Interpret the order, QQ, and direct-versus-assessed pattern jointly. Connect a supported finding to a managerial action and review condition. Document changes in a prediction, design, or interpretation. Explain a recommendation and its assumptions to a client. Reflect on the evidence needed to revise a judgment.

Pilot, repair, and freeze the collection plan before the main run.

## Module 5: Free Will and Axiomatic Agency

A beginner’s primer on ontology and conceptual engineering; reasons, reconsideration, control, and axiomatic choice models.

Distinguish a question about what exists from a question about how a concept should be used. Define agency through reasons, reconsideration, and effective control. Explain an axiom as an explicit condition on a model. Interpret the course’s choice patterns under their stated assumptions. Distinguish a modeled response pattern from evidence about human agency.

Collection opens under the frozen plan; develop an ungraded definition-and-evidence paragraph.

## Module 6: Agency over Time and Procedural Control

Immediate repetition and return histories; delegation, procedural authority, and welfare under explicit valuations.

Distinguish stable aggregate shares from individual revision. Read immediate and return histories using the correct denominator. Identify who can authorize, refuse, or revise a decision procedure. Calculate an illustrative welfare comparison under stated values. Recommend a procedural change with evidence and a review condition.

Collection closes; complete supplied agency practice and refine the recommendation.

## Module 7: Analysis and Peer Critique

Reconstruct results, troubleshoot validity problems, assess uncertainty, and improve the report and figures through peer review.

Reproduce a reported result from saved records and documented exclusions. Prioritize a design or analysis problem by its consequence for the claim. Show the comparison, denominator, units, and uncertainty in a figure. Evaluate a rival and the assumptions supporting an agency or welfare claim. Revise an argument in response to a specific peer critique.

Analysis, report and deck drafts, peer critique, and recorded revisions.

## Module 8: Presentations and Individual Defense

Explain and defend a managerial recommendation, respond to questions, and document what would change the decision.

Present a coherent decision, comparison, finding, and recommendation. Explain the full audit independently, including work completed by teammates. Distinguish observed evidence from conditional model and welfare claims. Answer a substantive challenge with evidence or a justified revision. Reflect on the development of one’s reasoning and contribution.

Team presentations and individual defenses; report, deck, and individual reflection due.

## Learning Activities and Assessment

Watch the relevant videos before the live meetings. Sixteen 90-minute sessions use cases, supplied calculations, workshops, critique, and presentations. The team project develops across the course: proposal in Week 3, collection-plan freeze in Week 4, collection in Weeks 5–6, analysis and peer critique in Week 7, and final submissions and defenses in Week 8.

The three individual discussions contribute 10% each, the team proposal 25%, the team report/deck 30%, the individual oral defense 5%, and the individual reflection 10%. Practice is ungraded.
