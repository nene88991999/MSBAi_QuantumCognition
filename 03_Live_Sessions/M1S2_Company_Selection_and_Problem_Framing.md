## Slide 2: The Decision Owner



Who must choose an action?

Which alternatives are feasible?

What evidence would change that choice?



### Discussion and Work

Write one sentence naming a decision owner and a concrete action. A broad topic such as AI in retail needs a narrower decision, such as how to order information in a product-selection survey.



## Slide 3: Evidence About the Setting



Identify two public sources.

Separate documented facts from your hypotheses.

Record the source, date, and relevant claim.



### Discussion and Work

Use the source ledger in the collection guide. A company announcement may establish a product feature; it need not establish a causal effect. Keep unsupported assumptions visible in the proposal.



## Slide 4: Two Competing Considerations



Explain why both considerations matter here.

Write a neutral question for each.

Keep the focal decision separate.



### Discussion and Work

Work on A, B, and F. Ask whether each consideration follows from the institutional setting. Define unfamiliar terms in the question itself. Avoid telling a synthetic respondent how the experiment is expected to turn out.



## Slide 5: A Research Question You Can Answer



Name the response procedure.

Name the focal outcome and comparison.

State the scope of the model-panel evidence.



### Discussion and Work

A usable question might ask whether the tested model gives different launch-support rates after two specified frame orders. Separate that question from whether real customers support launch. Draft the proposal research question.



## Slide 6: Scope and Feasibility



Check access, collection time, and approved budget.

Plan all six conditions on the same persona grid.

Choose a design you can document completely.



### Discussion and Work

Discuss feasibility with the instructor. The six conditions are detailed in the collection guide. A small pilot checks the workflow; it does not guarantee precise estimates. Record scope feedback before collecting project responses.



## Slide 7: Peer Review of the Proposal



Can a reader identify the management decision?

Do the frames follow from the public evidence?

Is the strongest alternative specific and testable?



### Discussion and Work

Exchange draft outlines. Give one substantive suggestion tied to a proposal rubric criterion. Each student decides how to revise their individual work.



## Slide 8: Your Judgment and AI Use



Write your reasoning before using AI assistance.

Log the tool, prompt, and resulting changes.

Explain what you accepted, revised, or rejected.



### Discussion and Work

Project Part 1 is AIAS 3. Follow the assessment instructions and keep the AI Attribution Log. The log should document judgment, not merely list a tool name.



## Slide 9: The Proposal Draft



Save the decision, sources, and three draft questions.

Record the scope feedback.

List the remaining design choices for Module 2.
