# Report and Deck Peer Review

M7S2 · 90 minutes

Watch the videos before the meeting. Bring a draft audit report, draft 6–10 slide presentation, reproducible figures, AI Attribution and contribution records, and the revision log.

**Work to take away:** Peer feedback, a revised report and presentation plan, and individual preparation notes for the oral defense.

## 0–10 Minutes · Read the Draft as a Client

| Question | Where should the answer appear? |
| --- | --- |
| What decision is at stake? | Opening and recommendation |
| What does the evidence support? | Results and uncertainty |
| What changes next? | Action and review condition |

Identify one missing link in the argument.

Exchange drafts with a team that has not followed the project closely. Students read silently before the authors explain. This tests whether the written argument stands on its own.

## 10–30 Minutes · Review the Claim and Its Support

| Check | Evidence to request |
| --- | --- |
| Comparison | Sample, procedure, and denominator |
| Model interpretation | Restriction and credible rival |
| Agency or welfare | Reasons, control, and value assumptions |

Write a precise question beside one unsupported sentence.

Use the existing report rubric. Distinguish descriptive findings, conditional model results, and stronger interpretations. Synthetic-panel outcomes should retain their model scope. Return uncertainty and practical thresholds to the main argument.

## 30–50 Minutes · Rehearse a Ten-Minute Client Story

Decision and comparison.

Evidence, uncertainty, and a credible rival.

Recommendation and the condition for revision.

Each team rehearses with a peer team. The 20-minute block allows a ten-minute presentation and ten minutes of feedback. The outline describes content; students should use connected explanations and meaningful visuals. The shared deck is normally 6–10 slides under the current brief.

## 50–65 Minutes · Practice an Individual Defense

| Prompt | What the speaker should explain |
| --- | --- |
| Why this comparison? | The decision and identifying conditions |
| What could overturn the verdict? | A rival test or new result |
| What did you revise? | Evidence and reason for the change |

Each member answers without teammate prompting.

This rehearsal is formative. The assessed oral defense in Week 8 is individual, with no live AI assistance or teammate prompting. Each student must understand the entire audit, including parts produced by others.

## 65–80 Minutes · Turn Feedback into a Revision

| Feedback | Action | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claim too broad | Narrow or add evidence | Match the observed comparison |
| Figure hard to read | Revise labels and display | Clarify units and uncertainty |
| Your feedback | Record the change | Explain its consequence |

Preserve the original claim in the revision log.

Teams prioritize substantive repairs before appearance. Each student keeps private reflection notes about how evidence changed their reasoning. The reflection remains individual; it should not be jointly written.

## 80–90 Minutes · Prepare the Final Week

Confirm the team presentation and each defense slot.

Submit report, deck, logs, and individual reflection in Week 8.

Use Canvas for exact dates and confirmed assessment rules.

No new grade percentages are introduced by this deck. Check file accessibility and that links work without private credentials. The reflection follows the existing brief, including its stated length, unless the instructor posts a revision.
