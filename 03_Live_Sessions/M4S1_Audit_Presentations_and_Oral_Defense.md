## Slide 3: A Figure for the Client Decision

What evidence belongs next to these rates?

Illustrative audit: 128/200 versus 102/200 valid responses.

| Category | Support for launch |
| --- | ---: |
| Convenience first | 0.64 |
| Privacy first | 0.51 |

**Units:** Support rate in the model panel. Values for percentage-formatted rate charts are stored as proportions (0.64 means 64%).

### Discussion and Work

This public preparation example uses imposed counts, not a student solution or a real company result. Students should identify the tested model and persona design, define each sequence, report the 13-point difference with an appropriate interval, and show how it affects the decision. The bars alone do not establish statistical precision or human customer behavior. In the client story, connect the decision, design, uncertainty, strongest alternative, and recommendation. This reference slide adds no time to the six 15-minute presentation slots.

## Slide 4: A Recommendation Depends on Costs

At what error cost would your recommendation change?

Imposed loss: A = 70 + 5c; B = 40 + 15c.

| Cost per error, c | Procedure A | Procedure B |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 75 | 55 |
| 2 | 80 | 70 |
| 3 | 85 | 85 |
| 4 | 90 | 100 |
| 5 | 95 | 115 |

**Units:** Total loss (illustrative units). Values for percentage-formatted rate charts are stored as proportions (0.64 means 64%).

### Discussion and Work

The horizontal variable c is the cost of one error of the type that occurs 5 times under A and 15 times under B. The fixed losses are 70 and 40 units. A and B tie at c=3 with loss 85. All costs and counts are imposed for oral-defense preparation. A manager must justify both counts and valuations. Students use their own evidence to name a review condition and identify the assumption that supports their broadest claim. The oral defense is AIAS 0.

## Slide 5: Averages and Subgroup Patterns

Which comparison deserves a question from the reviewer?

Imposed rates: 100 observations per profile in each order.

| Category | ABF | BAF |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Profile X | 0.8 | 0.4 |
| Profile Y | 0.4 | 0.8 |
| Pooled | 0.6 | 0.6 |

**Units:** F yes rate. Values for percentage-formatted rate charts are stored as proportions (0.64 means 64%).

### Discussion and Work

Equal group weights yield a pooled rate of .60 in both orders, while subgroup changes run in opposite directions. The four subgroup counts are 80,40,40,80 yes out of 100. This descriptive example does not supply intervals or establish subgroup effects. Ask about prespecification, uncertainty, and the relevance of subgroup definitions. Peer feedback should identify a supported claim and a missing link to the action. Students revise their individual reports using their actual evidence; this slide is a public preparation prompt.
