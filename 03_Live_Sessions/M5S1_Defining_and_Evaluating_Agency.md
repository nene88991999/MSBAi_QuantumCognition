# Defining and Evaluating Agency

M5S1 · 90 minutes

Watch the videos before the meeting. Watch the Module 5 introduction, L1, L1b, and L2. Read the philosophy primer. Bring the frozen team collection plan and one interface decision.

**Work to take away:** A defensible agency criterion, a counterexample, and a team paragraph separating the behavioral finding from its agency interpretation.

## 0–15 Minutes · What Kind of Claim is This?

| Statement | Question to classify |
| --- | --- |
| Agency is a capacity | What kind of thing exists? |
| The reminder changed clicks | What occurred under the procedure? |
| Users should be able to skip | What should be protected? |

Classify the claim and name the evidence it needs.

Students work individually for three minutes and compare in pairs. Introduce ontology, empirical evidence, epistemology, and normative inquiry in plain language. Categories can overlap. Ask how the claim would change if the skip option did not work.

Source basis: Nathan Yang, Free Will and a Choice-Theoretic Separation of Classical and Quantum Models, September 2026 revision; course philosophy primer. Background: Christian List, Decision Theory Presupposes Free Will (2024), https://philpapers.org/archive/LISDTP.pdf; Herman Cappelen, Introduction to Conceptual Engineering (2018), https://academic.oup.com/book/25598/chapter-abstract/192942115.

## 15–35 Minutes · Three Procedures One Setting

| Procedure | Who guides the result? |
| --- | --- |
| Random device selects off | A random rule |
| User considers study plans | The user’s deliberation |
| Service blocks direct access | The imposed interaction |

Propose a criterion; test it against all three cases.

Use groups of three, each defending one case. A predictable deliberate response may express agency. Randomness alone supplies no purpose or control. Ask what record would distinguish requested delegation from an imposed reminder.

## 35–50 Minutes · Apply One Ranking across Direct Questions

| Case | Focus off | Connection off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 70% | 60% |
| B | 70% | 40% |

If off has the higher fixed value, which case satisfies A3?

Teaching probabilities. Case A satisfies the binary standalone requirement with strict differences. Case B fails for the stated ranking. A change in a second answer alone is not the A3 comparison. Discuss what independent evidence would support the ranking.

## 50–60 Minutes · Evaluate a Definition of Agency

State the concern your criterion addresses.

Give a case your criterion handles badly.

Revise the criterion and identify a measurement.

Connect the exercise to conceptual engineering. A company may choose a convenient definition around existing controls. Require a reason for each criterion and evidence about effective operation, rather than counting visible buttons.

## 60–80 Minutes · Separate the Team’s Claims

| Claim | What supports it? |
| --- | --- |
| Behavioral pattern | Procedure, estimate, uncertainty |
| Agency interpretation | Reasons and procedural authority |
| Welfare implication | Independent outcome values |

Draft a paragraph that keeps these claims distinct.

Use the frozen team study as the starting point. A synthetic-panel audit concerns the model tested. Do not change collection arms to make an agency claim. Identify any additional human or valuation evidence as future work. Each member explains one inferential step.
