## Slide 2: Question Order in a Published Survey

What changed before the country question?

Pew Research Center, December 2008 U.S. poll.

| Category | Dissatisfied |
| --- | ---: |
| Country first | 0.78 |
| President first | 0.88 |

**Units:** % dissatisfied with the country. Values for percentage-formatted rate charts are stored as proportions (0.64 means 64%).

### Discussion and Work

Pew reports 78% dissatisfied when the country question came first and 88% when presidential approval immediately preceded it. These are published percentages of U.S. adults, not a new estimate. The page does not supply arm sizes here, so no confidence interval is inferred. Ask students to explain the stable-answer assumption and then name a possible contextual process. The pattern alone does not identify a quantum mechanism. Carry one assumption into Discussion 1.

## Slide 5: A Fair Comparison in the Survey Tool

The order of A and B varies. F stays last.

Which setting preserves the final decision?

Qualtrics documentation: question randomization.

[Official source image](assets/qualtrics_options.png)

**Image Description:** Official Qualtrics dialog shows no randomization, all-question randomization, a subset option, and advanced randomization. The advanced option is highlighted in the source.

### Discussion and Work

The screenshot is a documented product example, not this course survey. Have students draft the same convenience question A and privacy question B for both ABF and BAF. The launch-support question F stays last. Hold facts, response options, and respondent population fixed. Advanced randomization can preserve positions; do not assume randomizing every question implements ABF versus BAF. Explain how order will be assigned and recorded. Save the wording for the proposal.

**Sources:** [Source 1](https://www.qualtrics.com/support/survey-platform/survey-module/block-options/question-randomization/). Accessed November 11, 2026.

## Slide 6: A Fixed-Rate Benchmark

Two assessed action rates are 0.30 and 0.70.

Can any weighted average reach 0.80?

State the assumption that this comparison tests.

Imposed conditional rates: 0.30 and 0.70; weights range from 0 to 1.

| Weight on the 0.70 rate | Weighted rate |
| --- | ---: |
| 0 | 0.3 |
| 0.25 | 0.4 |
| 0.50 | 0.5 |
| 0.75 | 0.6 |
| 1.00 | 0.7 |

**Units:** Predicted rate. Values for percentage-formatted rate charts are stored as proportions (0.64 means 64%).

### Discussion and Work

Use the displayed line or the Module 3 notebook mixture helper. The values are imposed illustrations, not survey findings. A weighted average cannot exceed both fixed inputs. Applying this benchmark to another procedure requires carrying the conditional rates across procedures. Consider how a process-dependent alternative could change them.

Displayed values are imposed teaching examples.

## Slide 8: Predictions for a New Procedure

Which new observation would separate the models?

Imposed predictions. Both models match the earlier results.

| Category | Model A | Model B |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Earlier 1 | 0.5 | 0.5 |
| Earlier 2 | 0.6 | 0.6 |
| New procedure | 0.7 | 0.4 |

**Units:** Predicted yes rate. Values for percentage-formatted rate charts are stored as proportions (0.64 means 64%).

### Discussion and Work

The first two values are imposed earlier results that both models reproduce. The final values are competing predictions, not observations. A new procedure is held out when its results are kept separate during fitting and model selection. Ask students to name assumptions that justify their predictions, then write one comparison for their own proposal. These anonymous models illustrate prediction; neither is designated quantum or classical.
