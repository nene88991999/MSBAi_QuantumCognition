## 0–20 Minutes · Read the Histories

| History | What can be compared? |
| --- | --- |
| A then A | Immediate repetition |
| A then B then A | First and return answers |
| B alone and A then B | Target probability across procedures |

Which histories must belong to the same respondent?

Use the supplied practice data. For individual revision, first and return responses must be paired within one valid history. Across-condition rates can use comparable groups with appropriate uncertainty analysis. The worksheet uses constructed data and does not validate a psychological process.

Source basis: Nathan Yang, Agency and the Persistence of Choice, November 2026 draft; Agency Erosion, October 2026 draft; course philosophy primer. Practice data are constructed and are not observed human or model-panel results.

## 20–35 Minutes · The Same Totals Different Histories

| Category | Respondents |

| Off → On | 20 |
| On → Off | 20 |
| On → On | 30 |

Constructed histories for 100 respondents

Calculate net change and individual revision.

The supplied dataset has 100 complete histories. Initial and final off shares are 50%. Forty respondents switch, so net change is zero points and revision is 40%. This distribution is imposed for practice. No statistical or philosophical conclusion is drawn from it.
