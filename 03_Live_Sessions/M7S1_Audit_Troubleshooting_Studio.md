# Audit Troubleshooting Studio

M7S1 · 90 minutes

Watch the videos before the meeting. Bring the frozen plan, closed collection, executed notebook, issue log, and draft evidence table. Revisit the relevant Module 2–4 videos as needed.

**Work to take away:** Reproducible analysis, resolved or documented blockers, and figures with clear denominators, intervals, and interpretation.

## 0–10 Minutes · Choose the Issue That Blocks the Claim

| Issue | First diagnostic |
| --- | --- |
| Counts disagree | Trace identifiers and exclusions |
| Intervals look wrong | Check unit and resampling rule |
| Conclusion outruns evidence | Match the claim to the comparison |

Choose one issue and state its consequence.

Teams triage issues by their effect on validity, not by visual polish. The instructor circulates. If there is no blocker, audit a result another member produced.

## 10–30 Minutes · Reproduce One Result from Raw Records

| Stage | Evidence to show a peer |
| --- | --- |
| Raw data | Original calls and identifiers |
| Analysis sample | Rules and denominator |
| Result | Code, estimate, and uncertainty |

Can a teammate obtain the same number?

Use the approved notebook and protocol. Preserve provenance from raw records to a plotted result. Check that no retry is counted twice and that invalid cases follow the stated rule. Use a separate sensitivity analysis for necessary deviations.

## 30–45 Minutes · A Small Estimate Can Remain Imprecise

| Category | Estimate |
| --- | --- |
| Narrow interval | 2 |
| Wide interval | 2 |

Incomplete teaching figure: intervals of 1 to 3 and −5 to 9 points.

What additional display is needed before calling the effect small?

The chart intentionally supplies only two equal estimates. Students must add intervals on paper: narrow from 1 to 3 points; wide from −5 to 9. With a pre-specified ±4-point tolerance, only the narrow interval lies entirely inside. The missing intervals are the issue to identify. Values are constructed.

## 45–65 Minutes · Repair the Figure and Its Explanation

Show the comparison, denominator, and units.

Display uncertainty beside the estimate.

State the conclusion and the condition supporting it.

Teams revise a figure from their audit, not the teaching chart alone. Keep labels readable and avoid using color as the only distinction. Ask another team to explain the figure without the author’s narration. Check that the caption identifies model-panel evidence.

## 65–80 Minutes · Keep the Remaining Uncertainty Visible

| Unresolved item | Implication for the report |
| --- | --- |
| Missing required arm | Restrict the comparison |
| Model changed mid-run | Separate or qualify the results |
| Valuation unsupported | Report behavior; identify needed values |

Assign an owner and a resolution or limitation.

A documented limitation can be the correct outcome. Avoid filling a missing comparison with an unsupported assumption. Record repairs, timing, and consequences in the revision log. Protect the original prediction from hindsight rewriting.

## 80–90 Minutes · Prepare the Peer-Review Draft

Save the executed analysis and final figure data.

Draft the claim, rival explanation, and recommendation.

Bring unresolved questions to the next session.

Week 7 Session 2 uses peer critique of the report and deck. The team should bring a coherent draft, even when its conclusion is inconclusive. Assessment rewards reasoning and evidence, not support for a favored model.
