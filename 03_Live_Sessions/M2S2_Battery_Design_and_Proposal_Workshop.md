## Slide 2: The Decision and Question Battery



Read the decision statement aloud.

Check that A and B express distinct considerations.

Check that F records the decision of interest.



### Discussion and Work

A partner should be able to explain the design without additional background. Revise vague or technical wording. Preserve the same response coding across conditions.



## Slide 3: Six Conditions, One Persona Grid





| Condition | Question sequence | Context retained |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Isolated AB / BA | A, B, F / B, A, F | Fresh for each question |

| Constructed AB / BA | A → B → F / B → A → F | Prior questions and answers |

| Direct | F only | No prior assessment |

| Assessed | A → F | Prior question and answer |



### Discussion and Work

The isolated conditions ask every question in a fresh conversation. Constructed conditions retain actual prior answers. Direct and assessed arms supply the separate procedural comparison. An ordinary all-at-once panel is optional and does not replace a required arm.



## Slide 4: Facts, Coding, and Context



Hold the factual background fixed.

Use the same yes/no definitions.

Save the complete message history and retries.



### Discussion and Work

Walk through one hypothetical persona without making paid calls. Verify which messages would be present in each condition. A parser should reject unrecognized answers rather than silently treat them as no.



## Slide 5: The Collection Plan



Specify the persona grid and run schedule.

Use a randomized order of collection runs.

Record the model version and approved budget.



### Discussion and Work

The persona grid is a designed collection frame, not a probability sample of people. Document all fields, exclusions, and missingness. Budget for all conditions and any declared retry rule.



## Slide 6: Decision Rules



Specify important, small, and unresolved results.

Give a managerial action for each.

Separate operational action from a mechanism claim.



### Discussion and Work

Students draft their own thresholds and action branches. A flat or inconclusive result can support a sound recommendation. Grade depends on reasoning and evidence, not on obtaining a nonclassical-looking pattern.



## Slide 7: Peer Review Against the Rubric



Find one claim without enough evidence.

Find one design choice that could change the result.

Propose a concrete revision.



### Discussion and Work

Partners use the M2A2 rubric and collection checklist. Keep feedback specific. Each student owns the final proposal and discloses permitted AI assistance.



## Slide 8: Revisions and Their Reasons



Resolve the highest-impact issue first.

Record what changed and why.

Preserve the original prediction for comparison later.



### Discussion and Work

Allow quiet revision time. A useful revision log separates planned design changes from changes made after results are observed. The log later supports Project Part 2 and the reflection.



## Slide 9: Proposal Submission Check



Attach the proposal, battery, sources, and AI log.

Check the exact Canvas deadline.

Retain a fixed copy before collecting project data.
