# Pilot Evidence and Interpretation

M4S1 · 90 minutes

Watch the videos before the meeting. Watch Module 4. Bring the submitted team proposal, a small pilot or supplied demonstration, and the interpretation rules. Main collection has not opened.

**Work to take away:** A pilot issue log, checked interpretation rules, and a prioritized repair list for the Week 4 freeze.

## 0–10 Minutes · A Pilot Checks the Procedure

| Question | Pilot evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Was the right sequence asked? | Saved prompts and history |
| Can answers be coded? | Valid and invalid cases |
| Can the analysis run? | A reproducible output |

Which failure would stop full collection?

Distinguish the pilot’s implementation purpose from estimating a final substantive effect. Use supplied practice data if API access is unavailable. Collection begins in Week 5 after the freeze.

## 10–25 Minutes · Two Pilot Problems Can Look Similar

| Category | Valid responses | Invalid or missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Condition A | 95.0% | 5.0% |
| Condition B | 65.0% | 35.0% |

Illustrative rates; the categories total 100% within each condition.

What would you inspect before interpreting a difference?

Constructed teaching rates. Higher invalidity in B may reflect wording, formatting, or system behavior. Preserve all attempts and reasons for exclusions. Check whether failures depend on the response. Do not silently replace failures until the desired result appears.

## 25–40 Minutes · A Verdict Needs a Condition

| Evidence | Interpretation to examine |
| --- | --- |
| A clear order difference | Which fixed-response claim is challenged? |
| QQ interval includes zero | Which alternatives remain possible? |
| Wide effect interval | What practical claim is still unresolved? |

Add the assumption that makes each inference valid.

Teams apply their proposed thresholds. Nonrejection is not proof of absence. A small-effect conclusion requires the relevant interval to lie within the chosen practical tolerance. QQ alone does not select a unique mechanism.

## 40–60 Minutes · Troubleshoot the Pilot in Pairs

Trace one record from prompt to result.

Separate a design issue from a coding issue.

Explain the proposed correction before applying it.

Use a peer team as reviewer. Check context retention, unique run IDs, response parsing, model version, and planned resampling unit. Keep changes and their rationale in the revision log. Avoid changing a substantive rule because a pilot estimate looks inconvenient.

## 60–80 Minutes · Record What Must Be Repaired

| Problem | Consequence | Next check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Example: duplicate retry | Inflated denominator | Inspect run IDs |
| Your issue | State its consequence | Assign a verification |

Save a repair list with owners and evidence.

Teams fill their own rows. A repaired implementation must be rerun on a pilot. Store the reviewed proposal separately from the collection-ready protocol so changes are traceable. Do not delete prior versions from the project log.

## 80–90 Minutes · Prepare for the Freeze Meeting

Bring the repaired protocol and a replayable pilot.

State any unresolved risk and its owner.

Keep the final conclusions open until collection.

The next live session freezes the collection plan. No team presentation or individual oral defense is due in Week 4 under the eight-week schedule.
