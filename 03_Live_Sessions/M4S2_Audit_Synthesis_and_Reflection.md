## Slide 3: Three Audit Summaries

Which comparison is justified across these settings?

Imposed estimates and 95% intervals, in percentage points. Different settings.

| Audit | Estimate | Lower | Upper |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Audit 1 | 0.5 | -2 | 3 |
| Audit 2 | 1 | -12 | 14 |
| Audit 3 | 10 | 6 | 14 |

### Discussion and Work

Audits 1,2,3 have imposed estimates .5,1,10 and illustrative 95% intervals [-2,3],[-12,14],[6,14]. These are not class results, computed intervals, or a pooled estimate. First compare the model, persona grid, question wording, population, and outcome. Then consider effect size, precision, and each prespecified practical threshold. Replace the demonstration with volunteered class summaries during discussion. Do not interpret the number of positive intervals as a common effect.

## Slide 4: Costs of Two Review Procedures

How robust is the $100 difference to your assumptions?

Imposed costs: total $2,800 versus $2,700 per 1,000 decisions.

| Category | Fraud loss | Customer friction |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Current process | 2400 | 400 |
| Proposed process | 1800 | 900 |

**Units:** Cost per 1,000 decisions (dollars). Values for percentage-formatted rate charts are stored as proportions (0.64 means 64%).

### Discussion and Work

These are imposed expected costs for a workshop comparison, not measured returns or a recommendation for Stripe. Fraud losses fall by $600, while customer friction rises by $500. Customer friction means the cost of legitimate users facing extra checks, delay, or rejection. The total saving is $100 per 1,000 decisions. Ask what uncertainty or omitted cost would change the ranking and what a human pilot would need to measure. Compare the actions supported by the actual class audits.

## Slide 5: A New Test of the Strongest Alternative

What must stay fixed when you add a delay?

Imposed rival predictions; no outcomes from the proposed delay test.

| Category | Short-lived carryover | Persistent change |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Immediate answer | 10 | 10 |
| Answer after a delay | 2 | 10 |

**Units:** Predicted order difference (percentage points). Values for percentage-formatted rate charts are stored as proportions (0.64 means 64%).

### Discussion and Work

Both proposed explanations reproduce a ten-point immediate order difference. The first predicts decay after a specified delay; the second predicts persistence. These predictions are imposed conditional examples, not universal predictions of classical or quantum theory. A delay may also change memory, attention, or information. Specify its duration, hold other features fixed, and state assumptions that make the comparison informative. Students revisit the rival in their proposal and identify what evidence would change the recommendation.
