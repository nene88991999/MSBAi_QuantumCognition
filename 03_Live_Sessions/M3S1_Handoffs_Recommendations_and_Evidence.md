## Slide 3: Meridian’s Illustrative Console Choice

Conditional block rates: 0.63 and 0.41.

Fraud-judgment share: 9/11; assessed rate: 0.59.

Direct block rate: 0.69.

Fictional Meridian example; imposed rates, not field estimates.

| Procedure or conditional group | Block rate |
| --- | ---: |
| After fraud | 0.63 |
| After legitimate | 0.41 |
| Assessed | 0.59 |
| Direct | 0.69 |

**Units:** Block rate. Values for percentage-formatted rate charts are stored as proportions (0.64 means 64%).

### Discussion and Work

Meridian is fictional and these rates are illustrative. Ask students to calculate the weighted rate and identify what comparison is challenged by .69. Source: Nathan Yang, Case: The Intermediate Step; adapted to distinguish the fixed-rate benchmark from process-dependent alternatives.

Displayed values are imposed teaching examples.

## Slide 4: The Meaning of the Difference

Compute direct minus assessed.

State why 0.69 lies outside the fixed-rate bracket.

Name a process that could change conditional rates.

### Discussion and Work

The difference is .10 and the bracket is [.41,.63]. The weighted identity holds within assessed records. Carrying its rates to the direct procedure is the additional assumption. Ask groups to propose an informative comparison rather than assert that every classical explanation has failed.

## Slide 5: Equal Block Rates, Different Errors

Which outcomes matter beyond the block rate?

Imposed outcomes: 30 fraudulent and 70 legitimate attempts in each procedure.

| Category | Fraudulent | Legitimate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Procedure A | 25 | 15 |
| Procedure B | 10 | 30 |

**Units:** Payments blocked per 100 attempts. Values for percentage-formatted rate charts are stored as proportions (0.64 means 64%).

### Discussion and Work

Both procedures block 40 of 100 attempts. A blocks 25 fraudulent and 15 legitimate payments; B blocks 10 fraudulent and 30 legitimate payments. Fraud not blocked is therefore 5 versus 20. These are imposed workshop counts with known ground truth, separate from Meridian’s decision rates. A block rate alone does not measure accuracy or welfare. Students specify a managerial outcome and the validation evidence needed for their own setting.

## Slide 6: Listening and the Taste Profile

A play can serve a temporary purpose.

What outcome would show that recommendations improved?

Spotify track-exclusion announcement, October 2025.

[Official source image](assets/spotify2025.png)

**Image Description:** Spotify’s official track menu shows Hide in this playlist separately from Exclude track from your taste profile. The latter is highlighted in the original image.

### Discussion and Work

Spotify’s 2025 announcement documents a track-level exclusion control. The pictured distinction gives a real setting for Juno’s recommendation problem from the videos. Ask students to separate immediate playback, future recommendations, and the outcome the listener values. A temporary use does not by itself reveal a stable preference. Propose a comparison and a measure of recommendation quality; do not infer effectiveness or a quantum mechanism from the feature’s existence. Save the comparison for Discussion 3.

**Sources:** [Source 1](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-10-01/exclude-tracks-taste-profile/). Accessed November 11, 2026.
