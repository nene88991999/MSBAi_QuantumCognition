## Slide 3: Meridian’s Illustrative Console Choice



Conditional block rates: 0.63 and 0.41.

Fraud-judgment share: 9/11; assessed rate: 0.59.

Direct block rate: 0.69.



Fictional Meridian example; imposed rates, not field estimates.



| Procedure or conditional group | Block rate |

| --- | ---: |

| After fraud | 0.63 |

| After legitimate | 0.41 |

| Assessed | 0.59 |

| Direct | 0.69 |



### Discussion and Work

Meridian is fictional and these rates are illustrative. Ask students to calculate the weighted rate and identify what comparison is challenged by .69. Source: Nathan Yang, Case: The Intermediate Step; adapted to distinguish the fixed-rate benchmark from process-dependent alternatives.



## Slide 4: The Meaning of the Difference



Compute direct minus assessed.

State why 0.69 lies outside the fixed-rate bracket.

Name a process that could change conditional rates.



### Discussion and Work

The difference is .10 and the bracket is [.41,.63]. The weighted identity holds within assessed records. Carrying its rates to the direct procedure is the additional assumption. Ask groups to propose an informative comparison rather than assert that every classical explanation has failed.



## Slide 5: The Managerial Outcome



A block rate is one measured behavior.

Also consider fraud loss, false blocks, time, and reliance.

What evidence would justify changing the console?



### Discussion and Work

Groups design a production evaluation conceptually. Accuracy and welfare require outcome information; higher blocking alone is not success. Humr and Canan provide a related AI-supported decision study: https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/26/6/500. The classroom case is separate from that study.



## Slide 6: Juno’s Recommendation Problem



A feed can lead with popularity or a stated goal.

The displayed options and facts remain fixed.

How should the team evaluate a change in purchases?



### Discussion and Work

Juno is fictional. Ask whether purchase rate, later regret, goal attainment, and user control could lead to different judgments of the same design. Link the argument to Carroll et al., https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/carroll22a.html. Avoid equating engagement with welfare.



## Slide 7: What the Panel Represents

Tier 2 studies the model under documented prompts.

### Discussion and Work

Have each group rewrite an overbroad sentence into a claim about the observed unit. A synthetic persona is not a human respondent. Bisbee et al., https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2024.5, provide a comparison between synthetic outputs and human survey evidence.
