# Case Workshop

These cases adapt situations in Nathan Yang’s manuscript. The companies are fictional. The stated numbers are teaching inputs, and each exercise concerns a specific managerial decision. Use the cases to practice before applying the same reasoning to the publicly documented company in your project.

## Northwind: A Register or a Predictive Model?

Northwind’s analysts maintain a register of recurring judgment patterns. Each entry helps the team recognize a familiar problem and identify a response. A new survey procedure creates a pattern the register has not seen. The manager must decide whether another entry is enough or whether a model would improve future decisions.

Explain what the register already does well. Then choose a new question sequence and state a prediction before observing its results. Give a fixed-preference explanation, a classical process explanation, and a geometric model a fair opportunity to predict the same outcome. Specify what would count against each. The useful comparison concerns prediction under stated assumptions.

**Workshop output:** one prediction table and a paragraph explaining which future decision the comparison would inform. This prepares the reasoning used in Discussion 1 and Project Part 1. See [Module 1](M1_Judgment_and_Models.md).

## Meridian: The Intermediate Assessment

A payment console can request a block-or-allow decision directly or first require a fraud-or-legitimate assessment. In the illustrative assessed arm, block rates are 0.63 after a fraud judgment and 0.41 after a legitimate judgment. The fraud-judgment share is 9/11. The direct arm blocks at rate 0.69.

Calculate the assessed average. Identify whether the direct rate falls within the range of the two assessed conditional rates. State the additional assumption needed to apply that range across procedures. Then explain which transaction outcomes and error costs the manager would need before choosing a console. A block rate describes an action; accuracy requires knowledge of whether transactions were fraudulent.

**Workshop output:** a procedural comparison and a proposed outcome measure for a follow-up evaluation. The wiki’s bracket calculator and [Module 3 notebook](../06_Python/notebooks/M3_Interference_and_Brackets.ipynb) let you vary the inputs. The geometric notebook uses a separate imposed example and does not estimate the Meridian case.

## Juno: Recommendations and the Customer’s Goal

Juno’s shopping feed can begin with popular items or with a question about the customer’s goal. The company cares about purchases, but customers may also care about a budget, a planned use, or a longer-term objective. The presentation procedure may change which consideration guides the purchase.

Choose one customer goal and define an outcome that measures progress toward it. Design a comparison that keeps the items, prices, and factual information fixed while changing the sequence of the goal and popularity prompts. Consider a rival explanation based on attention or learning. Explain what evidence would favor that explanation and how the result would affect the product decision.

**Workshop output:** an outcome definition and a testable recommendation design. Use it to develop Discussion 3. [Carroll and colleagues](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/carroll22a.html) offer a related model of preference shifts.

## An AI Panel: The Boundary of the Evidence

A manager wants to use model-generated survey responses to investigate a real company decision. An analyst supplies order-specific rates, a QQ estimate, and a direct-minus-assessed comparison. All records come from one documented model and persona grid.

Write the strongest recommendation supported by those records. Name the owner and a condition for review. Then design the human comparison needed for any proposed use with customers. The panel can help develop and diagnose a procedure; its relevance to a human population is an empirical question.

**Workshop output:** a recommendation paragraph with the population and procedure stated beside the claim. This prepares Project Part 2 and the reflection. See [Module 4](M4_Evidence_and_Action.md).
