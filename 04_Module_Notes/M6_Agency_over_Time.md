# Agency over Time and Procedural Authority

A repeated A question tests immediate repetition. A–B–A records whether the answer changes when A returns after B. Preserve the paired history: unchanged group shares can conceal changes in both directions. Repetition and revision need interpretation through reasons, information, memory, and control.

Procedural authority includes choosing or refusing the preliminary interaction and revising permission before an action becomes binding. Informed, revocable delegation can support the user’s purposes. The final response frequency alone leaves the allocation of authority unresolved.

Consumer-welfare calculations need independently supported values. In the lecture’s constructed example, renewal probabilities of 10%, 20%, and 60% imply expected losses of $1, $2, and $6 when declining is worth $10 more than renewing. The comparison holds the offer and outcome values fixed and excludes differences in procedure costs. Authority can matter separately.
