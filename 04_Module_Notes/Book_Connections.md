# Connections to the Book

This companion adapts Nathan Yang’s *Quantum Cognition for Management* to the four modules of his MSBAi course. It introduces the book’s central decision problems, definitions, cases, and probability examples in the order used by the recorded lectures. The interactive wiki includes these explanations, a glossary, case exercises, reading links, and calculators.

## Course Path Through the Manuscript

| Course Topic | Manuscript Sources | Student Resource |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detection, construction, and model choice | Part I: the decision default, unified view, mathematical onramp, and Northwind case | [Module 1](M1_Judgment_and_Models.md), probability notebook, Northwind workshop |
| Sequence and model restrictions | Part II: order and QQ, evidence order; Python lab | [Module 2](M2_Order_and_Uncertainty.md), order calculator, classical counterexample |
| Intermediate judgments and recommendations | Part IV: the missing layer, handoffs, Meridian, and Juno | [Module 3](M3_Human_AI_Processes.md), interference calculator, case workshop |
| Model assessment and managerial decisions | Part VII: boundaries and synthesis; worked-audit appendix; Part VIII Python lab and synthetic-panel case | [Module 4](M4_Evidence_and_Action.md), audit notebooks, recommendation workshop |

The examples distinguish model calculations, hypothetical cases, and empirical findings. The probability calculator uses a two-dimensional projective model with imposed angles. The classical example shows why an order effect together with q = 0 does not select a unique mechanism. The bracket comparison states the assumption needed to carry conditional rates across procedures. Synthetic-panel conclusions remain tied to the model and collection procedure until relevant human evidence is available.

## The Broader Book

The manuscript also develops time and duration, organizational attention, strategic interaction, quantum game theory, concept combinations, and other extensions. Those topics extend beyond this four-module course. Its parts are:

1. Why Classical Models Break Down
2. Contextuality and Order Effects
3. Time and Lived Duration
4. Human-AI Decision Teaming
5. Organizational Attention
6. Strategic Interaction and Quantum Game Theory
7. Boundaries and Synthesis
8. From Theory to Practice

The [reading guide](../05_Readings/README.md) supplies an accessible entry into the course topics and optional mathematical depth. The wiki is a course adaptation of selected book content, rather than a complete reproduction of the manuscript. This focus lets students work through the core ideas within four weeks.
