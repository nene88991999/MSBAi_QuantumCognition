# Glossary

The examples use A and B for framing questions and F for a focal decision. A yes answer is coded 1 and a no answer 0. A question that was not asked has no answer and is recorded as `null`.

## Judgment and Models

**Detection explanation.** Asking a question records a relevant judgment that already exists. The explanation assumes the act of asking leaves that judgment unchanged.

**Construction explanation.** Answering helps form the judgment. Information, attention, and the preceding questions can matter.

**Model.** A simplified description that produces predictions. Its usefulness depends on the decision, assumptions, and evidence.

**Classical probability model.** A model assigning probabilities to specified events. Classical models can allow memory, learning, and context to change responses. A model with fixed, unaffected answers is one particular version.

**Quantum probability model.** A mathematical representation using states and questions as geometric objects. A question can update the state that determines later answers. This use of mathematics requires no assumption that the brain is physically quantum.

**State.** The model’s representation of a current disposition to answer. In the introductory example it is a unit vector, an arrow of length one.

**Projection and Born Rule.** A projection gives the component of the state in a question’s answer direction. Squaring its length gives the answer probability in the model.

**Projector.** A mathematical operation that extracts a component in a specified subspace. The introductory examples use orthogonal yes and no directions.

**Non-Commuting Judgments.** Mathematical judgment operations whose order affects the result. An observed order effect alone does not establish this mechanism.

**Joint Distribution.** Probabilities for combinations of outcomes, such as A yes and B no. A separate yes rate for each question does not determine their joint distribution.

**Held-Out Prediction.** A prediction evaluated on observations deliberately excluded from fitting or selecting the model.

## Comparisons and Uncertainty

**Order Effect.** A difference in a defined response rate between sequences. State the outcome and sequences explicitly.

**Frame Order.** The sequence of considerations or questions, such as convenience before privacy.

**Slate Order.** The arrangement of alternatives on a screen or list.

**QQ Statistic.** The difference between the two orders’ shares of matching A/B answers. The simple projective model predicts zero. Some classical processes do too.

**Conditional Rate.** A rate within a stated group, such as support for F among interviews that answered yes to A in the assessed condition.

**Fixed-Rate Bracket.** The range between two conditional rates. A weighted average of those fixed inputs must stay within that range. Applying the range to a different procedure requires an assumption that the inputs remain applicable.

**Interference Comparison.** In this course, the direct F rate minus the F rate after an intermediate assessment. It is a procedural comparison with several possible explanations.

**Estimand.** The quantity a study aims to estimate, including its outcome, population, and comparison.

**Confidence Interval.** An interval generated by a procedure intended to cover the target quantity at a stated frequency under its assumptions. It describes uncertainty in the estimate.

**Practical Tolerance.** A range of effects judged small enough for a stated decision. Specify it before viewing results and explain its units and rationale.

**Block Bootstrap.** Resampling complete groups of linked observations. The project keeps all six conditions for a persona together in every resample.

## AI Panels and Decisions

**Synthetic Respondent.** A model answering under a specified persona and prompt. It is not a sampled person.

**Persona Grid.** The documented set of persona descriptions used across collection conditions. A shared grid helps keep comparisons consistent.

**Isolated Condition.** Each question uses a fresh conversation. The model does not receive its earlier question-answer history.

**Constructed Condition.** The interview retains the actual preceding questions and answers. The retained context is part of the procedure.

**Tier 1.** A simulator with known rules, used to check the calculation.

**Tier 2.** Responses from an actual model under recorded prompts, versions, and settings.

**Tier 3.** Evidence from relevant people and the setting in which a decision will be used.

**Human Validation.** A comparison using relevant human outcomes to assess a claim about people. Similar demographic descriptions or aggregate averages do not establish it.

**Review Condition.** The evidence or event that should cause the owner of a recommendation to reconsider it.
