# Module 8: Presentations and Individual Defense

A recommendation becomes useful when another person can understand the decision, evaluate its support, and identify when it should change. This module brings the audit together in a team presentation and an individual explanation of the reasoning.

## Prepare the Argument

Watch the short Module 8 introduction and revisit the Module 4 material on communicating a recommendation. Open with the company decision and proposed action. Explain the comparison before interpreting the result. Show the uncertainty and a credible rival beside the evidence they qualify. End with a responsible person, a review measure, and a condition for revising the action.

Use meaningful figures whose labels can be read during the presentation. State whether a result comes from supplied practice data, a fitted model, or the collected synthetic panel. Keep the interpretation within that evidence. Where agency or welfare matters, identify whose reasons and control are relevant and which values support the comparison.

## Explain the Full Audit Independently

Each member prepares to discuss the entire audit, including work completed by teammates. Be able to reconstruct a statistic from its denominator, explain the uncertainty calculation, identify a plausible rival, and state what would change the recommendation. A useful answer connects the question to the evidence and explains the assumption supporting the inference.

When a question reveals a weakness, identify its consequence and revise the claim if appropriate. A carefully justified uncertainty or evidence gap can be the correct conclusion. The assessment rewards reasoning and evidence rather than a preferred model, sign, or philosophical position.

## Presentation and Submission

The team uses 6–10 slides for a ten-minute presentation. Every student then has a separate five-minute oral defense, with no live AI assistance or teammate prompting. Both Week 8 sessions contain scheduled slots. Follow the individual timetable in Canvas, including any overflow block.

One member submits the shared report, deck, executed notebook, data location, and attribution, contribution, and revision records by the end of Week 8. Identify all team members and provide the private repository link and submitted commit identifier. Use the [M4A1 brief](../02_Assessments/M4A1_Project_Part_2.md) for the full instructions and separate rubrics. The team report/deck contributes 30% of the course and the individual defense 5%.

## Reflect on the Reasoning

Each student writes the [individual reflection](../02_Assessments/M4A2_Reflection.md), worth 10%, by the end of Week 8. Explain an initial judgment, the evidence considered, a revision or justified retention, and your contribution and verification. The submitted prose must be your own under the brief’s AI Assessment Scale level 1 rule. You may use the later agency discussions to explain why you narrowed or retained an interpretation.

Read the short Module 8 conclusion and the course conclusion after the final work. The same habits apply to future decisions: define the comparison, make the assumptions explicit, examine alternatives, and preserve the evidence behind a revision.
