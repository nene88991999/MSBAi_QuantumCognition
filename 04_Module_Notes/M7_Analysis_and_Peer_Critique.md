# Module 7: Analysis and Peer Critique

A manager needs to know whether a reported difference is reliable enough to guide an action. The first task is to reconstruct the result. The next is to explain what it supports. This module uses the models and interpretive distinctions from Modules 1–6 to review a complete argument.

## Reconstruct a Result

Begin with a comparison that matters to the company decision. State which procedure each group received, which responses were included, and the denominator for the reported rate. Trace one displayed result back to the raw records. Check unique interview identifiers, invalid responses, and retries against the frozen rules. Have a teammate reproduce the calculation independently.

A figure should identify the comparison, units, denominator, and uncertainty. A small point estimate can remain too imprecise to support a small-effect conclusion. For example, suppose the practical tolerance is four percentage points in either direction. An estimate of two points with an interval from one to three lies inside that tolerance. The same estimate with an interval from minus five to nine leaves the classification unresolved. These are constructed values for interpretation practice.

## Review the Interpretation

Separate the observed response pattern from the assumption connecting it to a mechanism. An order difference challenges a specified fixed-response account under the design conditions. A QQ result checks a restriction of the chosen projective model. A credible classical process may remain compatible with both. The report should identify the surviving explanations and the observation that would help distinguish them.

Agency and welfare claims require the corresponding evidence. A synthetic panel describes the tested model, prompts, and persona grid. Its answers do not establish people’s reasons or effective control. A monetary welfare comparison also needs values and a defined benchmark. A useful recommendation may identify which human or valuation evidence should be collected next.

## Use Peer Critique

Give another team a coherent report and deck draft. Ask the reader to identify the company decision, main comparison, strongest evidence, rival explanation, and proposed action without an oral explanation from the authors. A specific question about one unsupported step is more useful than a general request for more detail.

Record the original statement, the feedback, the revision, and its reason. Preserve the original prediction and distinguish a planned test from an exploratory check. Prioritize corrections that affect validity before polishing appearance. Each member should be able to explain the full audit and cross-check another member’s work.
