# M1M1_Introduction

Welcome to Module 1. Let's begin with a simple example.

Imagine a financial app that asks a customer about her goals before it asks about risk. When the app opens with “protect my family,” she picks a low-risk option.

When it opens with “build wealth,” she picks a higher-risk one. So here's the puzzle: did the app uncover a preference she already had, or did the questions help her form it?

That difference matters, because the business uses her answer to recommend investments.

This module is called Why Classical Models Break Down. We'll start with a simple model in which questions just read answers that are already settled.

We'll call that detection: the question reveals a judgment that already exists. The alternative is construction: the interaction itself helps form the judgment.

To tell them apart, we need a benchmark, which is simply the result a model says we should expect. A benchmark gives us something concrete to compare with the data.

Before we test the quantum model, a short video will give you a picture of it, using an arrow and its shadows. Then we'll look at three patterns that the simple model struggles to explain.

The first is a result that falls outside the range the model allows. The second is answers that change when you change the order of the questions.

And the third is judgments about a whole, like a “pet fish,” that you can't work out from the parts alone. We'll take them one at a time, each with a business example.

For each pattern, we'll also look at other explanations, such as learning, memory, or uneven wording, because a surprising result can have more than one cause. By the end of the module, you should be able to explain what a model predicts, and what evidence would challenge it.
