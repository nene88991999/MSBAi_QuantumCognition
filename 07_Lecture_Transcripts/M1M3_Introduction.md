# M1M3_Introduction

Welcome to Module 3. So far, we've seen how questions, and the order we ask them in, can shape people's judgments.

Now let's add something many workplaces already use: an AI assistant. Imagine the AI summarizes a case, an employee reads the summary, and then the employee makes a decision, which may later be used to train the next AI model.

Notice what's happening here. The AI helps choose what the person thinks about before the decision is recorded.

That makes the AI part of the process that produces the answer.

This module is called Human-AI Decision Teaming. We'll start by comparing two ways of making a decision: acting directly, or first stating an assessment and then acting.

Then we'll look at recommendation feeds, like the ones in shopping apps, where the opening screen can draw your attention to one goal rather than another. These examples show why the interface, meaning the screen and the questions the person sees, belongs in the audit alongside the AI model's predictions.

We'll also introduce synthetic panels. A synthetic panel uses a language model to answer survey questions as if it were a group of fictional respondents.

We'll compare three ways of running one: giving the model all the questions in a single prompt, asking each question in a separate call, or holding a step-by-step interview where earlier answers stay in the conversation. Each design gives the model different information, so each can produce a different pattern of answers.

As we interpret those results, we'll keep one distinction clear: evidence about the AI model is not the same as evidence about people. A synthetic panel can help you test your questions and prepare a study with real people, but it can't replace that study.

By the end of this module, you should know which conclusion each kind of evidence supports, and what further study a business decision might need. Let's start with the AI-assisted review.
