# Conclusion to Module 7

We began this module with a result that seemed ready to guide a company’s decision. Checking the analysis showed why a persuasive recommendation needs more than an interesting difference. A reader must be able to follow the path from the original responses to the conclusion, including the choices made along the way.

Keep three questions together. What did we observe? State the comparison and the responses included in it. Show the uncertainty beside the estimated difference so the reader can judge its precision. What supports the interpretation? Identify the assumption that connects the observed pattern to the proposed explanation, and compare a credible rival. What would change the decision? Describe a further result or observation that would lead you to revise the recommendation. These questions also apply when the evidence leaves the main issue unresolved.

The agency material gives us another reason to make these distinctions carefully. A change in answers can motivate questions about reasons or control, but those questions require their own evidence. Peer critique helps us notice where an argument moves too quickly between these claims. Record the revision and the reason for it. In the final module, we turn to explaining the completed argument clearly and answering questions about the decision it supports.
