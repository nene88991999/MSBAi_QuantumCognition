# Introduction to Module 8

In the previous module, we checked the analysis and used critique to strengthen the argument. The next step is to explain that argument to someone who must decide what to do. A manager may understand the recommended action and still need to ask why the evidence supports it. Answering that question is part of the analysis.

Begin with the decision. Suppose a company is deciding whether to offer a direct route through a recommendation process. Explain whose choice is affected and what the proposed change would accomplish. Then show the supporting evidence. Identify the comparison, its uncertainty, and the strongest alternative explanation. If the evidence comes from a synthetic panel, explain that it describes responses from the tested model. Finally, state the condition for revision. Name an observation that would change the recommendation, such as users misunderstanding the direct route or the original response pattern failing to recur.

A careful question can reveal an assumption that was easy to miss in a written explanation. Take time to identify what is being asked, connect the answer to the evidence, and revise the claim when the reasoning requires it. Explaining the whole argument in your own words helps reveal which steps you understand and which still need work. The purpose is a recommendation that another person can evaluate and use.
