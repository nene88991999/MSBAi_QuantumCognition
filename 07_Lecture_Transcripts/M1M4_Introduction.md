# M1M4_Introduction

Welcome to our fourth module. By now, you know how to audit a decision process.

So the next question is a practical one: what should an organization actually do with the results? Imagine a client whose audit shows a large order effect.

Before they change anything, they need to know three things: why the effect happened, how sure we are about that explanation, and whose responses the evidence actually describes, real people or an AI model.

This module is called Audit Verdicts and Managerial Action. We'll begin by reading the three results together: the main effect, the QQ check, and the comparison between direct and assessed decisions.

Then we'll connect that pattern to the competing explanations. That matters because different causes call for different fixes.

Uneven wording, learning from an earlier question, and a real change in perspective each lead to a different response.

Next, we'll build recommendations that name a specific action and the person responsible for it. Each recommendation also needs what we'll call a review condition.

That's a result, or a cost, decided in advance, that would make the organization stop, reverse, or revise its decision. It keeps the action open to new evidence, instead of locking it in.

Finally, we'll look at how to write up and present the analysis for a client. We'll trace the reasoning from your first prediction to your final judgment, including any changes you made along the way.

By the end, you should be able to explain why an action follows from the evidence, and what would lead you to reconsider it. Let's begin by reading the three results together.
