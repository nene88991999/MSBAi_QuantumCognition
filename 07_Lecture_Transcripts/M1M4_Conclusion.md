# M1M4_Conclusion

That brings us to the end of Module 4. We've now followed an audit all the way from the first question to a recommendation a manager can act on.

It started with a decision the organization needed to make. Then we asked how the responses were produced, which explanation best fit the results, and how far the evidence could support an action.

A useful recommendation keeps all of those links visible. Say how much the outcome changed, and how precisely you measured it.

Read the pattern checks together, and compare them with the strongest rival explanation. Then name the action, and the person responsible for it.

Your client should be able to follow the reasoning, step by step, from the comparison to the change you're proposing.

We also talked about documenting how your thinking changed. Finding a smaller effect, a different explanation, or a narrower scope isn't a failure.

Each one can make the analysis better. What matters is pointing to the evidence that changed your view, and explaining how the recommendation changed because of it.

You can carry this approach into any decision. Say what you expect, test a prediction that could prove you wrong, and keep your conclusion tied to the model or the people you actually studied.

And include a clear condition for reconsidering the action. That gives an organization a decision it can make today, and a sensible way to respond when new evidence arrives.
